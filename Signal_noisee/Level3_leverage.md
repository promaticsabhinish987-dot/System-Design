# Chapter 3: Leverage

## Objective

Learn how to identify the few causes that produce the largest impact on an outcome.

This chapter answers:

> "Among all the causes, which one should I focus on first?"

---

# Core Principle

Not all causes matter equally.

Some causes have small impact.

Some causes have massive impact.

Reality is uneven.

```text
Many Causes
      ↓
Few Important Causes
      ↓
One Dominant Cause
      ↓
Outcome
```

Signal extraction is not just finding causes.

Signal extraction is finding the highest-leverage causes.

---

# Knowledge Gap

Most people think:

```text
All causes are equally important.
```

Reality:

```text
Some causes dominate outcomes.
```

---

### Example

Outcome:

Pass Coding Interview

Possible Causes:

* DSA Practice
* Communication
* Resume
* Sleep
* Luck

All influence the outcome.

But they do not influence it equally.

Possible Impact:

```text
DSA Practice      = 50%
Communication     = 20%
Resume            = 15%
Sleep             = 10%
Luck              = 5%
```

Largest leverage:

```text
DSA Practice
```

---

# Definition

## Leverage

### Definition

Leverage is the amount of outcome improvement produced by improving a variable.

---

### Formula

```text
Leverage
=
Outcome Gain
÷
Effort Invested
```

High leverage:

Small effort → Large result

Low leverage:

Large effort → Small result

---

# First Principle

If two causes affect an outcome:

Focus on the cause with greater impact.

---

Example:

Interview Success

Improve:

```text
Keyboard Brand
```

Impact:

Very low.

---

Improve:

```text
Problem Solving Ability
```

Impact:

Very high.

---

Therefore:

```text
Problem Solving
=
Higher Leverage
```

---

# Why Leverage Exists

Reality follows uneven distributions.

Small number of causes produce most outcomes.

This idea appears everywhere.

---

## 80/20 Principle

### Definition

Approximately:

```text
20% of causes
produce
80% of results
```

---

Examples:

### Software Engineering

20% of concepts:

* Variables
* Functions
* State
* Data Structures

produce most programming ability.

---

### Business

20% of customers often generate most revenue.

---

### Fitness

A few habits generate most results:

* Sleep
* Nutrition
* Progressive overload

---

# Highest Impact Variable (HIV)

## Definition

The variable that creates the greatest positive change in the outcome.

---

### Core Question

Ask:

```text
If I improve only one thing,
what creates the biggest gain?
```

This is the leverage question.

---

# Leverage vs Importance

Important does not always mean high leverage.

---

Example

Interview Success

Resume:

Important.

But once resume passes screening:

Further improvement has diminishing returns.

---

DSA:

Important.

And often high leverage.

Improving DSA may dramatically increase performance.

---

# Mental Model

```text
Variables
     ↓
Rank By Impact
     ↓
Highest Leverage Variable
     ↓
Maximum Outcome Gain
```

# Leverage Ranking Process

Step 1

Define outcome.

---

Step 2

List all causes.

---

Step 3

Estimate impact of each cause.

---

Step 4

Rank causes.

---

Step 5

Focus on top-ranked cause.

# High vs Low Leverage

## High Leverage

Characteristics:

* Large impact
* Repeated influence
* Changes outcome significantly
* Worth focusing on

Examples:

* DSA Practice
* Sales Conversion Rate
* Database Optimization

---

## Low Leverage

Characteristics:

* Small impact
* Cosmetic improvements
* Easy distractions

Examples:

* Logo color
* Keyboard brand
* Fancy productivity apps
